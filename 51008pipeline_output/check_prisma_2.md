## Thoracic endovascular aortic repair for retrograde type a intramural hematoma or aortic dissection with intimal disruption in the descending aorta: Systematic review and meta-analysis

Noritsugu Naito  and Hisato Takagi 

## Abstract

Background: While thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) for type B aortic dissection is widely used, its use in cases of type A acute aortic syndrome remains limited. This meta-analysis was conducted to determine the outcomes of TEVAR for retrograde type A intramural hematoma (IMH) and aortic dissection with intimal disruption located in the descending aorta.

Methods: Systematic searches were conducted up to April 2024. Pooled proportions of perioperative and late outcomes were computed. Additionally, standard mean differences (SMD) with 95% con fi dence intervals (CI) in aortic dimensions following TEVAR were calculated. Kaplan-Meier curves, derived from individual patient data extracted from the studies, were utilized to elucidate long-term all-cause mortality.

Results: Asystematic review identi fi ed 13 non-randomized studies encompassing 288 patients. Pooled proportions revealed short-term mortality at 1%, with incidences of spinal cord injury, stroke, new intimal tear/dissection in the ascending aorta, aortic rupture, necessity for aortic re-intervention, and late mortality each recorded at 1%, 1%, 3%, 1%, 6%, and 7%, respectively. Substantial reductions in the diameters of the ascending aorta, descending aorta, IMH/false lumen of the ascending aorta, and IMH/false lumen of the descending aorta were observed post-TEVAR (SMD [95% CI] = 1.11 [0.82 -1.39], 1.02 [0.58 -1.47], 3.06 [2.39 -3.74], and 2.77 [2.28 -3.26], respectively). The anticipated 5-year survival rate stood at 91.1%.

Conclusion: This meta-analysis suggests TEVAR may offer a viable and safe therapeutic option for retrograde type A acute aortic syndrome. Nevertheless, de fi nitive conclusions are hindered by the scarcity of available data.

## Introduction

Acute type A aortic syndrome, encompassing aortic dissection and intramural hematoma (IMH), represents a critical cardiovascular emergency leading to potential life-threatening complications, including aortic rupture, cardiac tamponade, and coronary ischemia. 1 Surgical intervention stands as the gold standard for managing this condition. Retrograde type A aortic dissection (RTAAD) or intramural hematoma (RTAIMH) is relatively infrequent entity characterized by the extension of the pathology from an intimal tear or disruption situated in the descending aorta. The complexity of such interventions underscores the non-negligible risks associated with open aortic repair. Conversely, thoracic endovascular aortic repair (TEVAR) has progressively emerged as a favored modality for managing both complicated and uncomplicated type B aortic dissections. 3,4 Irrespective of the approach (open vs endovascular) the fundamental tenet in treating acute aortic syndrome remains the exclusion of the intimal tear or disruption to prevent the progression of the disease and mitigate adverse remodeling of the untreated aortic segment. 3,5

Based on the anatomical similarity between type B aortic dissection and retrograde type A acute aortic syndrome, characterized by an intimal tear in the descending aorta, and recognizing that excluding the tear with a stent-graft can impede further disease progression, the utilization of TEVAR for RTAAD and RTAIMH has been reported in various studies. 6,7 However, comprehensive data regarding both perioperative and long-term outcomes, including assessments of aortic remodeling, remain scarce. Furthermore, concerns persist regarding stent-graft related complications, such as aortic rupture and the emergence of new intimal tears or dissections. In this comprehensive meta-analysis, we investigate the safety and ef fi cacy of TEVAR in patients with RTAAD and RTAIMH. Through the aggregation of pooled early and late outcomes subsequent to the procedure, our aim is to furnish insights that will not only inform clinical practice but also pave the way for future investigations aimed at delineating the optimal therapeutic approach for addressing these challenging subsets of aortic pathologies.

## Materials and methods

## Study design

This systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to the guidelines established by the Cochrane Collaboration and meticulously adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) guidelines. 8 PRISMA 2020 checklist is presented in Figure 1. A speci fi c review protocol does not exist for this analysis.

## Literature search

The author(s) declared the following potential con fl icts of interest with respect to the research, authorship, and/ or publication of this article: A thorough literature search was conducted using the electronic databases MEDLINE and EMBASE up to April 2024. The search strategy incorporated the following keywords: ( ' type A aortic dissection ' OR ' TAAD ' OR ' type A intramural hematoma ' OR ' type A IMH ' ) AND ( ' endovascular ' OR ' stent graft ' ) AND ( ' retrograde ' OR ' retrograde extension ' ). Two reviewers (N.N. and H.T.) independently carried out the search to identify relevant articles.

## Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The selection of articles adhered to stringent inclusion criteria, encompassing original studies reporting the short-term or long-term outcomes of the use of TEVAR for RTAAD or RTAIMH. Exclusion criteria included review articles, case reports, and investigations involving animal subjects. Additionally, studies not documented in the English language were excluded from consideration.

## Article selection and data extraction

The initial screening of articles was executed based on titles and abstracts, with potentially pertinent articles proceeding to a comprehensive full-text review. Subsequently, two reviewers (N.N. and H.T.) independently evaluated the full-text articles for eligibility, resolving any discrepancies through discussion and consensus. Data extraction was independently performed by the two reviewers, utilizing a standardized data extraction form. The extracted information from each included study included the author ' s name, year of publication, study design, sample size, characteristics of the study population, perioperative data (such as operation time, short-term mortality, spinal cord injury, stroke, new intimal tear or dissection in the ascending aorta, and aortic rupture), as well as rates of aortic re-intervention and mortality. Any discrepancies in data extraction were resolved through discussion and consensus.

## Data synthesis and statistical analysis

A systematic data synthesis and statistical analysis were undertaken to pool the data obtained from the included studies. The statistical analysis was performed using the R software (version 4.3.0, R Project; R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) employing the ' meta ' package. Appropriate statistical methods such as pooled proportion for perioperative outcomes and standardized mean differences (SMD) with 95% con fi dence intervals (CI) for changes in aortic dimension following TEVAR were employed. A signi fi cance threshold of p &lt; .05 was applied to determine statistical signi fi cance. A random-effects model was utilized for each outcome, irrespective of observed heterogeneity among the studies. Subgroup analyses were performed on studies exclusively focusing on IMH of the ascending aorta.

Individual patient data were reconstructed from Kaplan-Meier curves with number-at-risk tables, 9,10 utilizing the R package ' IPDfromKM ' as previously outlined. 11 If Kaplan-Meier curves with number at risk were not reported, event (death) and follow-up duration information for each patient were extracted. Subsequently, Kaplan-Meier curves were generated using the R packages ' survival ' . The expected survival rate at 5 year post-TEVAR was computed.

Evaluation of publication bias was conducted using funnel plots, which provide a visual representation of the distribution of study outcomes. Asymmetry in the funnel plots was assessed using the linear regression test, 12 employing modi fi ed standard error as suggested by Pustejovsky et al. 13 A signi fi cance level of p &lt; .1 indicated the presence of publication bias.

Heterogeneity between studies was assessed using the Cochran Q statistic, and the magnitude of heterogeneity was quanti fi ed by estimating I 2 . A signi fi cance level of p &lt; .10 and an I 2 value of 50% or more were deemed indicative of high heterogeneity.

## Risk of bias in individual studies

The Cochrane tool Risk of Bias Assessment Tool for Nonrandomized Studies 14 was employed to evaluate the quality of the studies in this meta-analysis. Two independent authors (N.N. and H.T.) conducted the assessment, resolving any discrepancies or con fl icts through consensus. The outcomes of the assessment were visually presented using the ' Robvis ' package in the R software.

## Ethical considerations

Given the nature of this study as a systematic review and meta-analysis of previously published articles, ethical approval was deemed unnecessary.

## Results

## Study selection

A comprehensive literature search yielded a total of 208 articles (Figure 2) relevant to the investigation of interest. Following exclusion based on abstract screening, 16 articles underwent comprehensive full-text review. Ultimately, 13 articles met the prede fi ned inclusion criteria and were included in the analysis, collectively encompassing a cohort of 288 patients. 6,15 -26 12 studies were retrospective, while one study was prospective observational study. Only one study 16 provided data on conventional open surgery as a comparative group, while the other studies exclusively focused on the patients who underwent TEVAR (single group).

## Study profile and patient characteristics

The study pro fi le, including study design, type of aortic disease (RTAAD or RTAIMH), time from onset to TEVAR, follow-up period, and exclusion criteria, is summarized in Table 1. Notably, seven studies exclusively focused on patients with IMH of the ascending aorta and fi ve studies exclusively focused on patients with type A aortic dissection. Perioperative patient characteristics are presented in Table 2. The time to TEVAR from presentation varied between studies. However, TEVAR was predominantly performed during the acute phase (&lt;14 days) or early subacute phase. In contrast, TEVAR during the hyperacute phase (&lt;24 hours) was rarely performed.

## Perioperative and postoperative characteristics

Table 3 provides details of the endovascular repair procedures. A variety of stent-graft devices were used across the studies, including both commercially available and custom-made devices. However, none of the studies utilized commercially available fenestrated or branched devices. For supra-aortic vessel reconstruction, techniques such as the chimney approach, physician-modi fi ed fenestration, and debranching procedures were employed.

Perioperative characteristics are shown in Figure 3. Pooled mean operation time was 81.5 minutes (95% CI; 70.2-92.8) (Figure 3(A)). The overall proportions for short-term mortality and perioperative complication were low. Short-term mortality was 1%, with incidences of spinal cord injury, stroke, new intimal tear or dissection in the ascending aorta, and aortic rupture each recorded at 1%, 1%, 3%, and 1%, respectively.

During the mean follow-up time of 46.4 months, the late mortality rate was 7% and the rate of aortic reintervention was 6% (Figure 4).

## Changes in aortic dimension following TEVAR

Positive aortic remodeling was observed following TEVAR (Figure 5). Signi fi cant reductions were noted in the diameters of the ascending aorta, descending aorta, IMH/false lumen of the ascending aorta, and IMH/false lumen of the descending aorta (SMD [95% CI] = 1.11 [0.82 -1.39] p &lt; .01, 1.02 [0.58 -1.47] p &lt; .01, 3.06 [2.39 -3.74] p &lt; .01, and 2.77 [2.28 -3.26] p &lt; .01, respectively). There was evidence of high heterogeneity of treatment effects among the included studies for these outcomes (I 2 &gt; 50% and/or p &lt; .1).

## Assessment of publication bias

Funnel plots were generated to assess data symmetry regarding post-TEVAR aortic dimension measurement changes (remodeling) (Figure 6). The funnel plot analysis yielded no signi fi cant evidence of publication bias for the examined postoperative factors ( p &gt; .1). The symmetric distribution of the data points suggests that there is no evidence of selective reporting, indicating that the included studies encompass a representative range of outcomes.

## Reconstructed Kaplan-Meier curves for all-cause mortality

The reconstructed Kaplan-Meier curves for all-cause mortality (Figure 7) were generated from individual patient data extracted from seven studies. 6,16,18 -20,22,23 Expected survival rates at 3 years and 5 years following TEVAR were 93.8% and 91.1%, respectively.

## Risk of bias

The risk of bias in the retrospective studies was assessed and summarized using a traf fi c-light plot, as shown in Figure 8. No studies were identi fi ed as having a serious risk of bias, while 12 studies were deemed to have a moderate risk of bias. The identi fi ed biases primarily stemmed from confounding factors and the selection of patients in the studies. While majority of the included studies were published in the late 2010s, several studies included cases from the late 2000s to early 2010s (Table 1). Advancements in stent-graft devices, surgical techniques and imaging studies providing accurate location of the intimal disruption over the decade might have played an important role in biases due to confounding factors.

## Discussion

The present meta-analysis aimed to investigate outcomes of TEVAR for retrograde extension of the intimal disruption in the descending aorta (RTAAD and RTAIMH) by calculating pooled early and late results from the included studies. To our best knowledge, this is the fi rst meta-analysis that focuses on the use of TEVAR in patients with these critical aortic conditions.

This study highlights the favorable short-term mortality and perioperative complication rates in patients undergoing TEVAR for RTAAD and RTAIMH with short operation time. Notably, pooled proportion for short-term mortality was only 1%, which is remarkably low compared with open surgery for conventional type A aortic dissection (15 -18%) 27,28 or IMH (7 -10%) 29 -31 reported in the previous studies. Furthermore, the rates of postoperative neurological complications (stroke and spinal cord injury) and aortic-related complications associated with stent-graft placement, such as new intimal tear/aortic dissection progression of the ascending aorta and aortic rupture were remarkably low at 1 -3 %. These favorable short-term outcomes might be attributed to short operation time, less invasive approach without sternotomy, and avoidance of using cardiopulmonary bypass with hypothermic circulatory arrest. However, the time from symptom onset to TEVAR varied across the included studies as seen in Table 1. TEVAR during hyperacute phase (&lt;24 hr) was performed in limited number of patients, who were deemed at high-risk of disease progression or were diagnosed with complicated type B aortic dissection, characterized by presence of hemodynamic instability, aortic rupture, refractory pain or organ malperfusion. The majority of the patients underwent TEVAR during subacute phase (more than 14 days after symptom onset). As they can wait for TEVAR in a stable condition, short-term outcomes might have been favorable in such patients. Regarding late outcomes during the mean follow-up time of about 3.9 years, the aortic reintervention rate and late mortality rate were also satisfactory at 6 and 7%, respectively. The survival rate calculated from reconstructed individual patient data was 93.8% at 3 years and 91.1% at 5 years. Late survival rate was also comparable in the patients of this study compared with patients who underwent open surgery for conventional type A aortic dissection (35 -70%at 5 years) 32 -34 and IMH (87-90% at 5 years). 31,35 Not only favorable survival outcomes, but positive aortic remodeling was observed both in the ascending aorta and descending aorta. Speci fi cally, the diameters of the ascending aorta and descending aorta were signi fi cantly decreased following TEVAR, and the thickness of false lumen or IMH was reduced signi fi cantly as well. As IMH and aortic dissection may have similar but different impacts on the outcomes, subgroup analysis of studies exclusively focusing on RTAIMHwasconducted. The subgroup analysis exhibited the same tendency of the positive aortic remodeling.

Considering anatomical similarity of intimal tear location between type B aortic dissection and RTAAD/ RTAIMH, selected patients with RTAAD and RTAIMH could be treated as type B aortic dissection. In other words, patients with complicated features should be treated during hyperacute or acute phase similarly to complicated type B aortic dissection. 3 Although, TEVAR during acute phase is associated with higher short-term complications, including new intimal tear and aortic rupture, possibly due to a more fragile and thinner dissection fl ap, 36 patients in three included studies 18,21,22 underwent TEVAR during the early phase (within 2 days). TEVAR may offer an alternative option to emergent open surgery in this population. On the other hand, patients without complicated features can undergo TEVAR during the subacute phase to mitigate the risk of procedure-related complications similarly to preemptive TEVAR for uncomplicated type B aortic dissection. TEVAR during the subacute phase still offers favorable aortic remodeling because aortic plasticity may be maintained up to 3 months from onset (subacute phase). 37,38

## Conclusion

TEVAR for RTAAD and RTAIMH with intimal disruption in the descending aorta has proven to be a promising option. However, conclusive determinations are hampered due to lack of comparative data. Further investigations are warranted to determine the optimal strategy for patients in these critical aortic conditions.
